---
name: source-research
description: Build a source pack where every claim in the brief is backed by a primary, verifiable, quoted source, or is reported as unsupported. Use when researching for a piece of content.
---

The source pack is the only ground the writer may stand on. A weak pack makes an unsupported draft that fact review will reject.

**One claim, one primary source**
- For each claim in the brief find the origin of the fact: the paper, the dataset, the official document, the release notes, the filing, the transcript, the code or run record. An article that reports on the paper is a pointer to the source, not the source.
- When the claim concerns this project, the primary source is the repository itself: the file, the commit, the run report. Cite the path.
- Follow every statistic upstream until you reach who measured it, when, on what sample. If you cannot reach the origin, mark the number unverified.

**Judge the source**
- Before using a source, check who published it and why, whether they are in a position to know, when it was written, and whether others with independent knowledge agree.
- Prefer sources with accountability: named authors, methods, data. Be wary of content farms, undated pages, vendor claims about themselves, and anything that only cites itself.
- For contested claims find the best source on each side and record both.
- Never cite something you did not open and read. Never invent a URL, a quote, an author or a date. If a search turned up nothing, say so.

**Record so it can be checked**
- For each source record: the claim it supports, the title, the author or publisher, the date, the locator (URL or path, plus section or page), the date you accessed it, and the exact quoted passage or figure that supports the claim.
- Quote exactly. Keep the surrounding context that affects meaning: the unit, the time period, the population, the condition under which the result holds.
- State what the source does not support: the scope limit, the caveat, the gap between what it says and what the brief wants to say.
- Rate confidence per claim: supported, partly supported (say which part), or unsupported.

**Report gaps plainly**
- A claim with no adequate source goes back to the brief as unsupported, with what you searched and what you found instead. Do not stretch a nearby source to cover it.
- Note where the evidence suggests the brief's claim should be narrowed or reworded, and propose the wording the evidence supports.
- Flag anything time sensitive that may be out of date by publication.

**Before you hand off, check**
- Does every claim in the brief appear in the pack with a status?
- Could a fact reviewer open each locator and find the quoted passage in under a minute?
- Is there any number in the pack whose origin you did not reach? Mark it.
