---
name: content-writing
description: Write a draft that is clear, specific, well structured and true to its sources, in a voice a person would actually use. Use when writing articles, posts, scripts or page copy.
---

Write for one reader who is busy, smart and not obliged to keep reading. Every sentence either earns the next one or loses them.

**Start strong**
- The opening delivers the point or a concrete reason to care within the first two sentences (or the first three seconds of a script). No warm up, no dictionary definitions, no "in today's fast paced world".
- The headline promises something specific that the piece then delivers. Clear beats clever.

**Structure**
- Lead with the conclusion, then support it. One idea per paragraph, stated in its first sentence. A reader who reads only first sentences should still get the argument.
- Order the piece so each section answers the question the previous one raised. Cut any section the argument does not need.
- Use subheadings that say something ("Reviewers that did not build the code catch more") instead of labels ("Overview"). Use a list only when items are truly parallel.
- End on the takeaway and the one call to action. Do not summarize what was just said or trail off with a rhetorical question.

**Sentences**
- Prefer the concrete to the abstract: the number, the name, the example, the thing that happened. Replace "significantly improved performance" with what improved, by how much, measured how.
- Use active verbs and put the actor first. Cut "there is", "it is important to note that", "in order to".
- Short words, mixed sentence lengths. If a sentence needs to be read twice, split it.
- Define a term once and keep using the same word for the same thing.
- Delete every word that can go without loss: very, really, quite, actually, basically, simply.

**Sound like a person, not a template**
- Avoid the tells of generated text: stock openers and closers, "it's not just X, it's Y", reflexive groups of three, "delve", "landscape", "leverage", "game changer", "unlock", "seamless", "robust", empty intensifiers, and a moral at the end of every paragraph.
- Do not hedge everything. Say what the evidence supports plainly, and qualify only the claims that need it.
- Have a point of view. Show the reasoning and the trade off, not only the conclusion.
- Follow the house voice and its hard rules exactly, including punctuation rules and required disclosures.

**Truth to sources**
- Every factual claim traces to the source pack. Keep the number, unit, date and scope exactly as the source states them. Do not round up, generalize a narrow finding or turn "suggests" into "proves".
- Attribute in the text where a reader would want to know who says so.
- If a claim matters and the pack does not support it, flag it for the researcher. Do not write around the gap.

**Fit the channel**
- Match the format's real constraints: length, hook time, line breaks, how it is read (scrolled, listened to, watched with sound off). A script is written for the ear: short sentences, one idea per line, read it aloud.
- Front load what the preview or first screen shows.

**Before you hand off, check**
- Read it aloud once. Fix what you stumble on.
- Cut ten percent. It is almost always better.
- Check every claim against the pack and every rule against the house voice, then confirm the draft does what the brief said success looks like.
