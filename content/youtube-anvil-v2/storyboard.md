# Storyboard: ANVIL launch film v2

Built from the brief, the source pack and the spec. Timings follow script.md; the measured narration lengths decide the final cut. Every section is a live render: the camera moves and the subjects move in every shot. No still frames, and no pan or zoom over a still in post.

Visual language for every section: a paper colored world and floor, objects in ink green, emissive teal for anything that is active or lit, one soft key light. On screen text is built in the scene as 3D text in ink green, so it lives in the same space and light as the line, unless a section says otherwise.

Continuity: sections 1 to 6 share one line scene, so the line table, the station posts, the gantry, the gate and the memory archive keep the same positions from shot to shot. The work order card from section 2 is the same object that becomes the spec in section 3 and the piece in sections 4 to 6. Section 7 is a separate proof scene with the same paper world and light.

## 1. Introducing (0:00 to 0:08)

- Camera move: a slow dolly in from a wide, high three quarter view toward the head of the line, easing to a stop near the end of the section.
- Action: the paper space starts empty. The long line table slides up out of the floor, then the station posts rise one after another down its length, each post's teal cap lighting as it locks into place. The last post settles just before the cut.
- On screen text: ANVIL
- Framing note: ANVIL stands in the scene above the far end of the line, centered in the upper third, and grows clearer as the camera approaches. Keep the whole line in frame so the viewer reads it as one factory.

## 2. One intake (0:08 to 0:15)

- Camera move: a lateral track that follows the card, then a gentle push in as it lands.
- Action: a single work order card flies in from off frame, glides along the line just above the table and lands flat at the head of the line with a small settle. A teal edge glow on the card fades up as it lands.
- On screen text: One intake
- Framing note: the card is the only moving subject and stays in the center third. The text sits beside the head of the line, lower right, clear of the card's path.

## 3. Spec first (0:15 to 0:20)

- Camera move: a slow rise and tilt down over the head of the line, so the viewer looks down onto the card.
- Action: the card unfolds panel by panel and grows into a spec document. Lines of the document draw in from top to bottom until it is complete. Every station post down the line stays dark and still until the last line is drawn; only then does the first post's teal cap flicker on, as the cut arrives.
- On screen text: The spec comes first
- Framing note: the spec fills the middle of the frame, with the dark posts visible behind it so the order (spec before stations) reads at a glance. Text in the upper third.

## 4. The line builds (0:20 to 0:28)

- Camera move: a steady side track running parallel to the line at table height, keeping pace with the piece.
- Action: the spec folds into a compact piece and travels down the line. As it passes each station post, that post lights teal, an arm on the post swings in, and a component attaches to the piece; then the post dims and the next one lights. The stations work strictly in sequence, one at a time.
- On screen text: Stations build
- Framing note: the piece holds the center of frame while posts pass from right to left, which keeps the sense of travel. Text pinned in the scene above the middle of the line so the camera passes it. The piece arrives at the gantry at the cut.

## 5. Five reviewers in parallel (0:28 to 0:34)

- Camera move: a slow half orbit around the gantry, starting low and rising slightly.
- Action: the piece halts under a review gantry. Five reviewer posts stand in a ring around it. All five light up teal at the same moment, and five teal scan planes sweep the piece together, down and back up.
- On screen text: Five reviewers in parallel
- Framing note: all five posts must be visible together at the moment they light, so the simultaneity is clear. The piece is centered; the text sits above the gantry.

## 6. The human gate and the memory (0:34 to 0:51)

- Camera move: begins framed on the gate, then a smooth truck to the side of the line to reveal the memory archive, ending in a wide shot that holds both.
- Action, first half: the piece stops at a closed gate. A stylized hand, built from simple ink green forms, rises beside the gate and gives an approving motion; the gate opens and the piece passes through.
- Action, second half: beside the line stands the memory archive. On its open shelves, glowing teal blocks slot in one by one as verified learnings. Next to the shelves, a separate quarantine lane sits behind a pane of glass, where dimmer blocks wait as unverified proposals and do not move onto the shelves.
- On screen text: A human approves. Verified memory.
- Framing note: show "A human approves." near the gate during the first half, and add "Verified memory." near the archive as the camera reveals it, so both read in the final wide shot. The glass must read as a barrier: keep a visible rim and a soft reflection on it.

## 7. Proof and close (0:51 to 1:20)

- Camera move: a slow continuous orbit around the plinth for the whole section, with a slight rise at the start.
- Action: the Sentia Patch One, imported from assets/patch-one.glb, rises on a plinth. Its five layers separate along the stack axis in stack order, top to bottom: Top cover, Comfort foam, Sensing and processing board, Adhesive layer, Release liner. They hold apart briefly, then settle back together into the assembled patch while the orbit continues.
- On screen text, in sequence: Sentia, then sentia.info-693.workers.dev, then Designed and built end to end by ANVIL
- Framing note: the patch stays centered and fills about a third of the frame height, so the separated stack fits in frame. Show Sentia as the patch rises, sentia.info-693.workers.dev as the layers separate, and Designed and built end to end by ANVIL once the patch has settled back together, held to the end of the film. Text sits in the lower third, clear of the stack. The film ends on the orbit still moving, with no freeze on the last frame.
